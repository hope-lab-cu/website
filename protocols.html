Microbial Culture Media, Colony Isolation, Purification and Preservation

Objective: Explain the roles of culture-media selection, colony isolation, purity assessment and preservation documentation in supervised microbiology research.

Method overview: Culture media are selected for the organism and the question being asked, then checked for performance and sterility before use. Isolation serves two purposes — streak-plate work separates individual colonies from a mixed sample, while spread-plate work is used for enumeration. Purity is judged from colony morphology and confirmed by subculture, and purified isolates are preserved with the records needed to maintain authenticated cultures.

Note: Operational culture conditions are governed by institutionally approved standard operating procedures.

02PDF · SUSCEPTIBILITY TESTING

Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST)

Objective: Summarize the principles, standardization requirements and reporting considerations for antimicrobial susceptibility testing.

Method overview: Susceptibility testing asks how well an antimicrobial inhibits a given organism, and gives a meaningful answer only when every step is standardised. Disk diffusion measures the inhibition zone produced as an antimicrobial diffuses into an inoculated agar plate, while dilution methods determine the minimum inhibitory concentration in liquid or agar media. Interpretation uses organism-specific criteria, each run includes reference quality-control strains, and results are reported with the criteria applied and the limits of the method used.

Note: Does not reproduce proprietary breakpoint tables or provide an executable AST procedure. The current CLSI standard should be consulted for interpretation.

03PDF · PROBIOTIC CHARACTERISATION

LAB Autoaggregation and Coaggregation Assays

Objective: Describe in-vitro aggregation characteristics relevant to the phenotypic evaluation of probiotic lactic acid bacteria.

Method overview: Aggregation is a phenotypic property examined alongside other probiotic characteristics. In the autoaggregation assay a standardised cell suspension is left undisturbed and its optical density is followed over time, giving the proportion of cells that settle out of suspension. In the coaggregation assay two strains are mixed in a defined ratio and the measurement is compared against each monoculture, with matrix controls and replicate readings throughout.

Note: Limitations apply when inferring adhesion-related properties from in-vitro aggregation.

04PDF · EMULSIFICATION ASSAY

Emulsification Index (E24) Assay

Objective: Estimate the proportion of a liquid mixture represented by a stable emulsion after 24 hours.

Method overview: The E24 assay estimates how much of a liquid column remains a stable emulsion 24 hours after a test material has been mixed with oil. Pre-prepared, cell-free, non-viable material is combined with a selected food-grade oil, mixed under defined conditions and left to stand; the emulsion layer is then measured against the total liquid height and expressed as the emulsification index. Matched matrix and positive reference controls are run alongside, and replicates are documented.

Note: Interpretation limits apply to the E24 value under the conditions of the assay.

05PDF · SEQUENCE ANALYSIS

16S rRNA Amplicon Analysis with QIIME 2 and DADA2

Objective: Infer amplicon sequence variants and characterize taxonomic composition and microbial community diversity from processed 16S rRNA sequence data.

Method overview: The workflow begins after sequencing, with digital FASTQ files and a manifest that is validated before import. Sequences are quality-inspected and denoised with DADA2, which resolves amplicon sequence variants rather than clustering at an arbitrary similarity threshold. The resulting feature table supports taxonomic classification and diversity analysis, with control samples reviewed before results are accepted and commands, parameters and software versions retained for reproducibility.

Note: Starts from digital FASTQ files and excludes wet-lab sample processing.

06PDF · ANTIOXIDANT ASSAY

DPPH Radical Scavenging Assay for Antioxidant Activity

Objective: Estimate in-vitro radical-scavenging capacity using the DPPH colorimetric assay.

Method overview: The DPPH assay is colorimetric — a stable radical reagent loses its colour as it is reduced by antioxidant compounds in the sample, so the fall in absorbance is used as a proxy for radical-scavenging activity. Reliable results depend on a sample blank for the sample's own colour, a reagent blank, and a positive antioxidant reference run alongside. Scavenging is calculated from blank-corrected absorbance, and samples are normally read across a concentration series rather than from a single point.

Note: Reports in-vitro radical-scavenging capacity under the conditions of the assay.

07PDF · ENZYME SCREENING

Hydrolytic Enzyme Screening: Protease, Pectinase, Cellulase and Amylase

Objective: Summarize substrate-specific approaches for evaluating extracellular hydrolytic enzyme-associated activity.

Method overview: Screening for extracellular hydrolytic enzymes is substrate-driven: each enzyme class is paired with a substrate it acts on and a way of making that action visible on a plate. Protease is typically examined with a protein substrate such as skim milk or casein, pectinase with pectin, cellulase with a cellulose derivative and amylase with starch, each developed so that degradation appears as a clearing or halo around the colony. Growth context, blank and reference controls and consistent halo reporting all matter.

Note: Plate-based screening indicates substrate-associated activity under the test conditions; it does not by itself establish enzyme identity or yield.

USING THESE REFERENCES

Questions about these references

Are these protocols complete standard operating procedures?

No. They are method and standards references retained for student use in research and training. Operational culture conditions and laboratory procedures are governed by institutionally approved standard operating procedures.

Do these references reproduce CLSI breakpoint tables?

No. The antimicrobial susceptibility testing reference introduces disk-diffusion and dilution-based AST, organism-specific interpretive criteria and reference quality controls, but it does not reproduce proprietary breakpoint tables or provide an executable AST procedure. The current CLSI standard should be consulted for interpretation.

Can I use these methods in my own research?

The documents are shared here for convenient access by HOPE Lab members and students. The protocols may have been adapted to specific organisms, samples, instruments and experimental objectives, and their suitability and reproducibility under different conditions have not necessarily been established.
